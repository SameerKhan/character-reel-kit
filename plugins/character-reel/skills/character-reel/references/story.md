# Story: the two-hander format

## Measured reference

The format copies the rhythm of the "Claude & Pixel" reels (an AI character and a human) without copying their world.
Measured on the three highest-reach reels of that series:

| Measure | Reference |
|---|---|
| Turns per reel | 26 to 33, 1 to 8 words each |
| Speech rate | about 2 words a second overall; panic lines up to 6.9 w/s, deadpan around 2 w/s |
| Gaps between turns | 0.6 to 0.8 s; comic holds 1.5 to 2.3 s |
| Shot length | about 2.0 s (25 to 33 shots a reel) |
| Captions | one static phrase at a time |
| Characters | near-still (15 to 23% of frames have no motion) |
| Line-to-line pitch | 12 to 27 semitones per character (panic high, deadpan low) |

## Structure that worked

1. **Confession hook in under 3 s**: the human admits the disaster to the AI ("Hey Switch, did I post my lunch on
   the client's LinkedIn?").
2. **The AI's deadpan answer makes it worse** ("Yes. You did." / "It's doing numbers.").
3. **Absurd specifics escalate** (900 likes on the lunch, 12 on the launch post).
4. **A hold on the listener** for the worst reveal.
5. **One product idea resolves it**: the AI explains WHY the disaster worked, using one real capability.
6. **The human repeats the mistake as the closer**, and the AI gets the last word ("That's a wrap.").

## Rules (each one came from a rejected version)

- **One causal product idea.** Version 5 listed four capabilities in eight turns and the owner said "too many
  features, the script gets lost". The reference uses one capability, in about three turns, late, and it solves the
  human's problem.
- **The AI does not do chores on screen** ("Drafted.", "Scheduled.") unless that is the one idea.
- **The human's goal never changes** until it is resolved.
- **Every claim traces to a source** (a product brief, a help page). Record it in `claims_checked_against`. Rejected
  in review: claims the product cannot back, quantified time savings, implied auto-publishing.
- **Short lines need context words for some engines.** One- and two-word lines were the most fragile in every TTS
  tried; with ElevenLabs dialogue they are fine, but avoid a lone name ("Switch.").
- **End on a callback**, not a sentiment.

## Copy the format, not the world

Reviewers flagged trade dress when the first versions copied the reference's look. The example uses its own world:
a giant phone lying on a social media manager's night desk, a ring light, sticky notes, a mug, a soft brand sign.
